Stage 1 · The Depressed Brain

Depression Changes Brain Chemistry

Depression is linked to disrupted chemical signaling across the brain — particularly reduced activity of serotonin, a neurotransmitter tied to mood, energy, and motivation. Scroll in to see exactly what goes wrong at a single connection between two neurons.

Stage 2 · The Solution

This Pill Is the Solution

Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) are the most common medication for depression. Rather than adding more serotonin, they change how an existing signal gets recycled at the synapse — as you're about to see up close.

Stage 3 · Delivery

Into the Brain

The pill dissolves and enters the bloodstream, traveling all the way down to a single connection between two neurons — the synapse you're about to see up close.

Stage 4 · Normal Signaling

Serotonin Crosses the Gap

A presynaptic neuron releases serotonin into the synaptic gap. The molecules drift across and bind to receptors on the neighboring neuron, carrying the "feel good" signal forward.

Stage 5 · Depression

The Reuptake Pump Intervenes

A reuptake pump on the presynaptic neuron rapidly reabsorbs serotonin before it can reach the other side. Less signal gets through — contributing to the mood and energy symptoms of depression.

Stage 6 · Treatment

An SSRI Blocks the Pump

A Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (SSRI) molecule enters the gap and docks directly onto the reuptake pump, physically jamming it and stopping the early reabsorption.

Stage 7 · Restored Signaling

Serotonin Lingers & Signals

With the pump blocked, serotonin stays in the gap far longer, giving it time to reach and activate many more receptors — restoring healthier mood signaling over time.
